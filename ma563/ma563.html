MA563: Introduction to Differential Geometry.

Fall 2026

This is a proof-based introduction to differential geometry, beginning with curves and surfaces in Euclidean space and continuing to intrinsic geometry and Riemannian manifolds. A syllabus can be found here.

The course text is Wolfgang Kühnel, Differential Geometry: Curves–Surfaces–Manifolds, third edition.

Final project

Work in pairs on one of the topic sheets below. Each sheet contains five connected problems exploring ideas beyond our lectures through proofs, computations, and examples. The project is worth 25% of the course grade.

  • Written solutions: submit one joint 5–10-page, cleanly typeset PDF generated using LaTeX. Address all five problems, organized by problem number. This is a polished solution write-up, not a separate expository essay.
  • Mathematical expectations: define notation, justify your arguments, and cite sources where used, with a short bibliography. Give proofs unless the sheet explicitly permits quoting a result; distinguish conjectures and numerical evidence from proofs.
  • Presentation: give a joint 20-minute talk, followed by brief questions. Highlight a central result, a proof idea, and an illuminating example. Both partners should speak and understand the entire project.

Choose a topic

Each PDF includes the five problems, submission instructions, and suggested references.

  • Smooth manifolds (PDF)

    Charts, tangent spaces, and the connection between intrinsic manifolds and embeddings.

  • Vector bundles (PDF)

    Local trivializations, global frames, the Möbius bundle, and the hairy-ball theorem.

  • Geodesics and Clairaut’s relation (PDF)

    Conserved quantities and geodesics on cones, spheres, and tori.

  • Lie groups (PDF)

    Rotations, tangent matrices, Lie brackets, and the matrix exponential.

  • Differential forms and Stokes’ theorem (PDF)

    Closed and exact forms, local potentials, and how integrals detect holes.

  • Minimal surfaces (PDF)

    First variation of area, minimal graphs, and the catenoid–helicoid family.
